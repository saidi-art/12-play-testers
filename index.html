Questions developers actually ask

The blunt answers, including the one every skeptic asks first.

Do you need access to my Play Console?

No. You send your closed-testing tester link, a contact email and any test instructions. That's the whole handoff — we never touch your console.

Why 12 testers for 14 days?

Google requires personal developer accounts to run a closed test with at least 12 opted-in testers for 14 consecutive days before applying for production access. Uninstalling doesn't reset the opt-in, and low engagement can get a production request rejected — which is exactly what we manage against.

What happens if a tester goes quiet?

They're replaced at no extra cost. The daily monitoring exists to catch that early, so your count and engagement never slip during the window.

Do I ever talk to the testers?

Only if you want to. All tester communication runs through us by default; you get a managed run and a quiet inbox.

How do we start?

Order your package, send the tester link, contact email and instructions, and we take it from there. Work starts once the fee is received.

Is using a service like this against Google’s rules?

Google’s requirement is real, engaged testers on real devices, continuously opted in for 14 days. That is exactly what we provide: hired testers with genuine devices and activity. What puts accounts at risk is fake accounts, emulators or coordinated installs, and we manage against all three.

How fast does the run start?

Hiring starts as soon as your fee is received. We place your 12 testers into your closed test and confirm once all 12 are opted in; the 14-day window starts from there.

Do I need 12 testers for every new app?

Yes. Production access is gated per app. Once an app has production access, updates to that same app don’t need another test run.

Do testers need to be in my target countries?

No. Google doesn’t require closed testers to be in specific countries, so your testers can be anywhere.

What happens after day 14?

You apply for production access, with our support through the application. If Google pushes back, we keep following the run until validation, however long it takes.
